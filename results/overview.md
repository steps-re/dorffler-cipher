These decoded cipher passages from the 1680s despatches of Simon Dörffler, Swedish resident in Poland, to King Charles XI document Polish domestic politics, military campaigns, and foreign diplomacy across four volumes. Throughout the correspondence, Dörffler frequently pleads for his unpaid salary and expenses to cover high living costs, travel, and diplomatic gifts (19/100 p0028, 19/101 p0204, 19/102 p0421, 19/102 p0588).

In Volume 19/100, the despatches initially track Imre Thököly's rebellion in Upper Hungary and Ottoman military advances (19/100 p0251, 19/100 p0277, 19/100 p0279). At the Polish court and Diet, intense struggles emerge between the Imperial faction, led by Baron Zierowski, and the French and Brandenburg factions (19/100 p0280, 19/100 p0321, 19/100 p0326). A major scandal erupts when intercepted letters expose treasonous collusion between Crown Treasurer Morsztyn and French envoy de Vitry (19/100 p0326, 19/100 p0333, 19/100 p0350). Despite factional opposition, Poland ratifies an alliance with the Emperor and prepares to relieve besieged Vienna (19/100 p0338, 19/100 p0350, 19/100 p0403). Concurrently, Dörffler negotiates a general Swedish-Polish alliance and secret plans for Swedish troops to transit Lithuania into Ducal Prussia against Brandenburg under the pretext of fighting the Turks (19/100 p0294, 19/100 p0393, 19/100 p0402).

Volume 19/101 covers subsequent campaigns against Ottoman and Tatar forces in Ukraine, Podolia, and Hungary, highlighting Cossack operations, leadership changes, and subsidies (19/101 p0084, 19/101 p0086, 19/101 p0339, 19/101 p0357). Polish-Austrian relations cool due to Imperial marriage politics with Bavaria, encouraging renewed French intrigues under the Marquis de Béthune, including marriage schemes for Prince Jacob (19/101 p0212, 19/101 p0219, 19/101 p0277). Dörffler also notes unrest in Courland and Pilten, where nobles consider seeking Swedish protection (19/101 p0320). The volume concludes with extensive records of the Polish Diet debating treasury audits, papal subsidies, currency counterfeiting, and provincial tax tariffs to pay the army (19/101 p0495, 19/101 p0502, 19/101 p0535, 19/101 p0544).

In Volume 19/102, ongoing frontier warfare against the Tatars and Turks near Kamieniec accompanies joint military discussions with Muscovy, though Russian campaigns against Perekop prove inconclusive (19/102 p0148, 19/102 p0173, 19/102 p0487, 19/102 p0541). Pilten deputies again privately offer to recognize Charles XI as their feudal lord (19/102 p0328). Polish hostility toward Austria and Brandenburg escalates after Princess Radziwiłł marries the Count Palatine of Neuburg, snubbing Prince Jacob and dominating the 1689 Diet (19/102 p0388, 19/102 p0418, 19/102 p0463, 19/102 p0503). Factional clashes involving the Lithuanian hetmans and bishops, alongside intercepted diplomatic mail and the Queen's financial intrigues, ultimately rupture the Diet (19/102 p0467, 19/102 p0474, 19/102 p0502).

Volume 19/103 opens with another prolonged and chaotic Diet, where bishops propose beverage excise taxes to fund the army (19/103 p0033, 19/103 p0035, 19/103 p0036). Despite magnate conspiracies and fierce clashes between Imperial ministers and Béthune, marriage terms are negotiated between Prince Jacob and a Palatine princess (19/103 p0010, 19/103 p0017, 19/103 p0064, 19/103 p0197). Meanwhile, devastating Tatar raids continue across Volhynia and the southeastern border alongside rival Ottoman peace rumors (19/103 p0064, 19/103 p0258, 19/103 p0306). Following another broken Diet at Grodno, the sickly Polish King struggles to prepare for new assemblies amidst noble competition for vacant offices (19/103 p0403, 19/103 p0503).
